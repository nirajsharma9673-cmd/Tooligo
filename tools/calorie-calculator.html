How It Works

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is BMR multiplied by an activity factor that accounts for exercise and movement. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor formula , which is considered more accurate than older equations (like Harris-Benedict) by most modern nutrition guidelines.

Mifflin-St Jeor Formula:

  • Male: BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a + 5
  • Female: BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a − 161
  • TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Where w = weight (kg), h = height (cm), a = age (years).

Worked Example: A 30-year-old male weighing 75 kg at 178 cm, moderately active:
BMR = 10(75) + 6.25(178) − 5(30) + 5 = 750 + 1112.5 − 150 + 5 = 1,718 calories/day
TDEE = 1,718 × 1.55 = 2,662 calories/day

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain basic functions like breathing, circulation, and cell production. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) adds the calories burned through exercise and daily movement. TDEE is what you need to maintain your current weight; eat fewer calories for weight loss, or more for weight gain.

Why is sex a required input?

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula uses different constants for males and females because men and women typically have different body composition and metabolic rates. Men generally have more muscle mass and higher BMR, so the formula includes a +5 adjustment for males and a -161 adjustment for females to account for these differences.

How accurate is this calorie estimate?

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is a population average equation and is accurate to within about 10-15% of measured BMR for most individuals. Actual calorie needs vary based on genetics, body composition, hormone levels, and other individual factors. These estimates are a helpful reference point but should not be treated as exact prescriptions.

How does activity level affect the multiplier?

Each activity level corresponds to a multiplier that estimates the additional calories burned through physical activity. A sedentary person (x1.2) burns only their BMR plus minimal movement, while an extremely active person (x1.9) may burn nearly double their BMR. Be honest about your activity level for the most useful estimate.

Is this medical advice?

No. This calculator provides general reference estimates based on a standard population formula. It is not a medical diagnosis, dietary prescription, or substitute for professional advice. For personalized nutrition or medical guidance, please consult a qualified healthcare provider.
