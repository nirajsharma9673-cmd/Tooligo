Tax Comparison — FY 2026-27

How It Works

Income tax in India follows a progressive slab-based system: the more you earn, the higher the tax rate on each portion of your income. You do not pay a single rate on your entire income — instead, each slab of income is taxed at its corresponding rate.

Section 87A Rebate: If your total taxable income (after deductions) falls within the rebate threshold, you may qualify for a full rebate on your tax liability, making your tax effectively Rs 0. For the New Regime (FY 2026-27), the threshold is Rs 12,00,000 with a maximum rebate of Rs 60,000. For the Old Regime, the threshold is Rs 5,00,000 with a maximum rebate of Rs 12,500.

This calculator shows both regimes side by side so you can compare your tax liability before choosing.

Worked Examples

New Regime (Salaried, Rs 15,00,000 income): Gross Income = Rs 15,00,000. Standard Deduction = Rs 75,000. Taxable Income = Rs 14,25,000. Tax: Rs 0 (0–4L) + Rs 20,000 (4–8L at 5%) + Rs 40,000 (8–12L at 10%) + Rs 33,750 (12–14.25L at 15%) = Rs 93,750. Since taxable income exceeds Rs 12L, no 87A rebate applies. Cess (4%) = Rs 3,750. Total Tax Payable: Rs 97,500.

Old Regime (Salaried, Below 60, Rs 15,00,000 income, Rs 1,50,000 80C): Gross Income = Rs 15,00,000. Standard Deduction = Rs 50,000. 80C Deduction = Rs 1,50,000. Taxable Income = Rs 13,00,000. Tax: Rs 0 (0–2.5L) + Rs 12,500 (2.5–5L at 5%) + Rs 1,00,000 (5–10L at 20%) + Rs 90,000 (10–13L at 30%) = Rs 2,02,500. No 87A rebate (income exceeds Rs 5L). Cess (4%) = Rs 8,100. Total Tax Payable: Rs 2,10,600.

In this example, the New Regime saves you Rs 1,13,100.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the old and new tax regimes?

The New Tax Regime (introduced in Budget 2020) offers lower tax rates but eliminates most deductions and exemptions. The Old Tax Regime has higher rates but allows deductions under Section 80C, 80D, HRA exemption, home loan interest, and others. Since FY 2023-24, the New Regime has been the default — if you want to use the Old Regime, you must specifically opt for it when filing your return.

Which deductions are only available in the old regime?

Deductions exclusive to the Old Regime include: Section 80C (investments up to Rs 1.5L), 80D (health insurance premiums), 80E (education loan interest), 80G (donations), HRA exemption, LTA (Leave Travel Allowance), standard deduction (Rs 50,000), and home loan interest under Section 24. The New Regime only allows a standard deduction of Rs 75,000 for salaried individuals and a few specific deductions like employer’s NPS contribution (80CCD(2)).

Is the new tax regime the default now?

Yes. Since FY 2023-24, the New Tax Regime is the default for all individual taxpayers. If you wish to be taxed under the Old Regime, you must specifically opt out. Salaried individuals without business income can switch between regimes each year. Business income taxpayers must choose one regime and stick with it, barring certain conditions.

Can I switch between old and new regimes every year?

Yes, if you are a salaried individual or non-business taxpayer, you can choose the regime that benefits you most each financial year. However, if you have business or professional income, you can switch only once, and once you opt for the New Regime, you cannot revert to the Old Regime in a subsequent year (except in specific situations).

What is Section 87A rebate?

Section 87A provides a full rebate on your tax liability if your total taxable income (after deductions) does not exceed the specified threshold. For the New Regime (FY 2026-27), the threshold is Rs 12,00,000 with a maximum rebate of Rs 60,000. For the Old Regime, the threshold is Rs 5,00,000 with a maximum rebate of Rs 12,500. If your tax before rebate is less than these limits, the rebate brings your tax to Rs 0.

Does this calculator save or share my data?

No. All calculations happen entirely in your browser. No data is sent to any server, saved to disk, or shared with third parties. You can refresh the page or close your browser and nothing is retained.

What isn’t covered by this calculator?

This calculator does not account for surcharge (applicable above Rs 50 lakh income), marginal relief, capital gains tax, business or professional income, presumptive taxation, agricultural income, foreign income, double taxation relief, or all possible deductions beyond Section 80C and a general “other deductions” field. For accurate tax planning covering these areas, please consult a chartered accountant.
