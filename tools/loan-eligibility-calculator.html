How It Works

This calculator uses the FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) method — the most common approach Indian banks use to determine personal loan eligibility. Here’s the logic:

  1. Affordable EMI: (Monthly Income × FOIR / 100) − Existing EMIs. This is the maximum EMI you can comfortably pay.
  2. Reverse EMI Formula: Given an affordable EMI, the formula calculates the maximum loan principal: P = EMI × [(1+r)n − 1] / [r × (1+r)n], where r = monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100) and n = tenure in months.
  3. No Eligibility: If your existing EMIs already equal or exceed your FOIR-based capacity, the calculator shows a “No Eligibility” warning.

Worked Example

Monthly Income: Rs 60,000 • FOIR: 50% • Existing EMIs: Rs 5,000 • Rate: 10.5% • Tenure: 5 years (60 months)

Step 1: Affordable EMI = (60,000 × 50%) − 5,000 = 30,000 − 5,000 = Rs 25,000.

Step 2: Monthly rate r = 10.5 / 12 / 100 = 0.00875. n = 60. P = 25,000 × (1.0087560 − 1) / (0.00875 × 1.0087560) = Rs 11,63,523 (approx).

Step 3: Total payment = 25,000 × 60 = Rs 15,00,000. Total interest = 15,00,000 − 11,63,523 = Rs 3,36,477.

What is FOIR?

FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) is a metric lenders use to assess your repayment capacity. It represents the percentage of your monthly income that can be allocated toward loan EMIs. While the FOIR limit varies by lender and loan type:

  • Salaried individuals: Typically 50–55% FOIR
  • Self-employed: Typically 40–50% FOIR
  • Home loans: Some lenders allow up to 60% FOIR

A lower FOIR means more conservative lending, but it also ensures you don’t overburden yourself with debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is FOIR and how is it used?

FOIR stands for Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio. It is the maximum percentage of your monthly income that banks allow toward loan EMIs. For example, if your income is Rs 50,000 and the bank uses a 50% FOIR, your total EMIs (including existing ones) cannot exceed Rs 25,000 per month. This ensures you have enough income left for living expenses after paying EMIs.

What if my existing EMIs are zero?

That’s great! Your affordable EMI will be at its maximum — simply (Monthly Income × FOIR). Having no existing obligations makes you eligible for a higher loan amount. Use this calculator to see exactly how much you qualify for with different interest rates and tenures.

How can I improve my loan eligibility?

You can improve eligibility by: (1) increasing your income, (2) reducing or closing existing loans, (3) choosing a longer tenure (reduces EMI but increases total interest), (4) improving your credit score (a higher score may get you better FOIR), or (5) adding a co-applicant with income.

Does this include home loan eligibility?

Yes, this calculator can estimate home loan eligibility as well — simply adjust the interest rate and tenure to match current home loan rates (typically 8.5–10.5% p.a.) and long tenures (15–30 years). Keep in mind that home loan FOIR may be higher (up to 60%) for some banks and may vary based on the applicant’s age.

Will I get the exact amount shown here?

This calculator provides a strong estimate based on the FOIR method used by most Indian banks. However, the final loan amount depends on several other factors including your credit score (CIBIL), employment stability, bank-specific policies, and verification of documents. The result should be used as a planning tool, not a guarantee.
