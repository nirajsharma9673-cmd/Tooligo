About SIP

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed amount regularly in mutual funds. This calculator helps you project your investment returns.

How to Use

  1. Enter your monthly investment amount
  2. Enter expected annual return percentage
  3. Enter your investment period in years
  4. Click Calculate to see results

Benefits of SIP

  • Regular investment discipline
  • Rupee cost averaging
  • Power of compounding
  • Flexibility in amount
  • Long-term wealth creation

How It Works

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount regularly (usually monthly) in mutual funds. The calculator uses the future value formula for a series of regular investments compounded monthly:

M = P x [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] x (1 + r)

Where P = Monthly Investment, r = Monthly Return Rate (annual return / 12 / 100), n = Total Number of Months. The final (1 + r) factor accounts for investing at the start of each month rather than the end.

Worked Example

Scenario: Invest Rs 5,000 per month for 10 years at 12% expected annual return.

Step 1: Monthly rate r = 12 / 12 / 100 = 0.01. Total months n = 10 x 12 = 120.

Step 2: M = 5,000 x [((1.01)^120 - 1) / 0.01] x 1.01 = Rs 11,61,695 (Final Amount).

Step 3: Total invested = 5,000 x 120 = Rs 6,00,000. Investment gains = 11,61,695 - 6,00,000 = Rs 5,61,695.

You can also enable the step-up option to increase your monthly investment by a fixed percentage each year — a great way to grow your savings as your income grows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a realistic expected return?

Expected returns vary by asset type. Equity mutual funds historically return 12-15%, balanced funds 8-10%, and debt funds 6-8% annually.

Can I change my SIP amount?

Yes, most SIP plans allow you to increase or decrease your investment amount. Check with your investment provider.

Is this calculator accurate?

Yes, we use the standard SIP formula. Actual results may vary based on market conditions and actual returns.

What happens if markets fall?

SIP helps in rupee cost averaging. You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

Should I stop SIP during market downturns?

No, continuing SIP during downturns can help you buy more units at lower prices, potentially increasing long-term returns.
