How It Works

This calculator uses established guidelines from medical and fitness authorities to estimate your optimal daily water intake. The calculation follows three simple steps:

  1. Base Intake (from body weight): Weight (kg) × 0.033 = base water in litres. This is a widely used rule of thumb based on the principle that your body needs about 33 ml of water per kg of body weight.
  2. Exercise Adjustment: (Exercise minutes / 30) × 0.4 litres. For every 30 minutes of physical activity, add approximately 400 ml (about 1.5 glasses) to replace fluids lost through sweat.
  3. Climate Adjustment: If you live in a hot/humid climate, total intake is increased by 15% to account for increased sweating and fluid loss.

Total = (Base + Exercise) × Climate Factor. The result is shown in litres and standard 250 ml glasses for easy tracking.

Worked Example

Weight: 70 kg • Exercise: 45 min/day • Climate: Hot/Humid

Step 1: Base = 70 × 0.033 = 2.31 L

Step 2: Exercise = (45 / 30) × 0.4 = 0.60 L

Step 3: Subtotal = 2.31 + 0.60 = 2.91 L. Climate factor = 1.15 (multiplier for hot climate).

Total: 2.91 × 1.15 = 3.35 L = approximately 13 glasses of water per day.

Why Hydration Matters

Proper hydration is essential for virtually every bodily function, including temperature regulation, joint lubrication, nutrient transport, and organ function. Even mild dehydration (1–2% of body weight) can impair cognitive performance, mood, and physical endurance. Chronic dehydration has been linked to kidney stones, urinary tract infections, and constipation. Individual needs vary, so use this calculator as a starting point and adjust based on your thirst, activity level, and how you feel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 8-glasses-a-day rule accurate?

The “8×8” rule (eight 8-oz glasses, about 1.9L) is a common recommendation but it’s a general guideline, not a scientific standard. Actual water needs vary significantly based on body weight, physical activity, climate, and individual metabolism. This calculator provides a more personalized estimate than the one-size-fits-all 8-glass rule.

Does this include water from food and other beverages?

The calculator estimates total water intake from all sources combined. As a general guide, about 20–30% of your daily water typically comes from food (fruits, vegetables, soups, etc.) and other beverages like tea, coffee, and milk. You can adjust your drinking water target down by roughly 20% if you consume a diet rich in water-containing foods.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes. Overhydration (water intoxication or hyponatremia) occurs when you drink so much water that your body’s sodium levels become dangerously diluted. This is rare for most people and typically only occurs with extreme consumption (multiple litres in a short period, often during endurance sports). For the amounts recommended by this calculator, overhydration is not a concern for healthy individuals.

Should I drink more if I’m pregnant or breastfeeding?

Yes. Pregnant women generally need about 2.3–3.1 litres per day, and breastfeeding mothers need about 3.1–3.8 litres per day — higher than the standard recommendation for non-pregnant adults. Use this calculator as a baseline and consult your healthcare provider for personalized advice during pregnancy and lactation.
